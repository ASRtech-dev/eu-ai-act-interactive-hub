Priority 01 • Articles 5, 50, 85 & 86 + Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR)

Your Rights as a Citizen in Europe

Clear, enforceable legal protections shielding you from manipulative AI, unmarked deepfakes, and unfair automated decisions in daily life.

Article 5 • Banned Since 2 Feb 2025 01
Art. 5 • Biometric & Scoring Shield

8 AI Practices Banned to Protect You

AI systems that manipulate your choices, exploit vulnerable groups, or score your personal life are outlawed across all 27 Member States.

No Social Scoring No Workplace Emotion Scans No Mass Face Scraping
Chapter II • Article 5(1), points (a)–(h) • What AI Cannot Do to You
  • Hidden Manipulation (Art. 5(1)(a)): No subliminal or deceptive AI that tricks you into harmful decisions.
  • Targeting Vulnerability (Art. 5(1)(b)): No exploiting children, older people, disabilities, or financial hardship.
  • Social Scoring (Art. 5(1)(c)): No public or private citizen rating based on behaviour or personal traits.
  • Crime Prediction by Profiling (Art. 5(1)(d)): No predicting crime risk based solely on personality or profiling without objective facts.
  • Untargeted Face Scraping (Art. 5(1)(e)): No mass-harvesting photos from social media or CCTV to build face databases.
  • Emotion Tracking at Work & School (Art. 5(1)(f)): Banned in workplaces and classrooms (allowed only for strict medical/safety use).
  • Sensitive Biometric Labelling (Art. 5(1)(g)): No deducing race, politics, union membership, religion, or sexual orientation from biometrics.
  • Live Public Facial ID by Police (Art. 5(1)(h)): Banned in public spaces except with a prior judicial warrant for kidnapping, terrorism, or severe Annex II crimes.

Article 50 • Right to Know 02
Art. 50 • Authentic AI & Watermarks

Your Right to Spot AI & Deepfakes

You must always be told when you are chatting with an AI bot or looking at AI-generated voice, video, images, or news.

Upfront Chatbot Notice Deepfake Labels C2PA Watermarks
Chapter IV • Article 50(1)–(4) • Builder (Provider) vs. User Organisation (Deployer) Duties
  • Chatbots Must Identify Themselves (Art. 50(1) — Provider): AI builders must design chatbots so you know upfront you are talking to a machine, not a human.
  • Digital AI Watermarks (Art. 50(2) — Provider): Generative AI tools must embed machine-readable watermarks (like C2PA provenance data) into synthetic audio, images, video, and text.
  • Permitted Biometric/Emotion Notice (Art. 50(3) — Deployer): If a permitted emotion or biometric system is used, you must be informed beforehand under GDPR rules.
  • Visible Deepfake & AI News Labels (Art. 50(4) — Deployer): Publishers must clearly label deepfakes and AI-written public-interest news (unless reviewed and approved by a human editor).

Arts. 85–86 & GDPR 03
Arts. 85–86 • Human Review & Redress

Your Right to Explanation & Redress

If high-risk AI affects your job application, loan, school admission, healthcare, or public benefits, you can demand clear reasons and file a complaint.

Art. 86 Clear Explanation Art. 85 Formal Complaint GDPR Human Review
Chapter IX, Section 4 • Articles 85–86, Art. 27 FRIA & GDPR Rights
  • Demand a Clear Explanation (Art. 86): You have the right to receive a clear, meaningful explanation of how a high-risk AI system influenced a decision about you.
  • File a Formal Complaint (Art. 85): Anyone can submit a reasoned complaint to any national Market Surveillance Authority if an AI system breaks the law.
  • Mandatory Rights Check Before Launch (Art. 27 FRIA): Public bodies, banks (credit scoring), and life/health insurers must complete a Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment before using high-risk AI.
  • Full GDPR Protection Still Applies (Arts. 6, 9 & 22): Your personal data requires a valid legal basis and you keep your right to human intervention over purely automated decisions.

GDPR Art. 28 • Protected Workplace Prompts

Safe Workplace Prompts & AI Outputs

Never paste personal data or company secrets into public AI chatbots without a GDPR Article 28 contract — and remember that 100% AI-generated work has no EU copyright.

No Personal Data Without Art. 28 DPA Zero-Training API Mode Human Authorship Needed
See workplace prompt Do’s/Don’ts & copyright rules
  • Keep Client & Staff Data Private (GDPR Art. 28): Never paste names, emails, or HR records into external LLMs without a signed Data Processing Agreement (DPA).

Arts. 4, 16–27 & 57 02
Arts. 4 & 57 • Staff Training & SME Sandboxes

Staff AI Training, Roles & Startup Sandboxes

Since 2 February 2025, every employer using AI must train its staff (Art. 4), know whether it is an AI Builder or User Organisation (Arts. 16–27), and can test innovations in free SME Sandboxes (Art. 57).

Art. 4 Staff AI Training Art. 25 Role-Shift Rules Art. 57 Free SME Sandboxes
See Builder vs. User duties & Art. 25 triggers
  • Mandatory Staff AI Literacy (Art. 4): Every provider and deployer must train staff and contractors to understand AI limits, hallucinations, and risks for affected people.
  • AI Builders (Providers, Art. 16) vs. User Organisations (Deployers, Art. 26): Builders handle QMS (Art. 17) and CE marking (Art. 48); Deployers ensure human oversight, keep logs for ≥ 6 months (Art. 26(6)), and notify workers before workplace use (Art. 26(7)).
  • When a User Becomes the Builder (Art. 25(1)(a)–(c)): You take on full Art. 16 Provider liability if you (a) rebrand a high-risk AI under your own name, (b) substantially modify it, or (c) turn a general AI/GPAI tool into a High-Risk system (original providers must assist under Art. 25(2) unless they banned high-risk use).
  • Free Startup Sandboxes (Art. 57): Every Member State must open at least one AI Regulatory Sandbox by 2 August 2026 with free priority access for SMEs and startups.

01 List Your AI Tools & Role Arts. 3, 16, 25 & 26

Map every AI tool your team uses and check if you are a Deployer (Art. 26) or become a Provider (Art. 16) by rebranding or fine-tuning for high-risk tasks (Art. 25(1)(a)–(c)).

02 Check Risk Level & Art. 6(3) Filter Arts. 5–6 & Annexes I, III

Block all Art. 5 banned AI uses immediately and check if your tools fall under Annex I (regulated products) or Annex III (HR, credit, education, public services), documenting any Art. 6(3) non-material risk exemption.

For High-Risk AI, assign Art. 14 human oversight, keep automatic logs for ≥ 6 months (Art. 26(6)), inform workers before launch (Art. 26(7)), and complete an Art. 27 FRIA where required.

Label chatbots and deepfakes with Art. 50 / C2PA watermarks, affix Art. 48 CE marking to high-risk AI, and register in the Art. 49 EU Database before launch.

How the 4-Tier Risk System Protects You

The higher the risk to people, the stricter the rules — most everyday AI stays free of red tape, while high-impact AI faces strict safety checks.

Tier 01 • Art. 5 Banned (8 Red Lines)
Tier 02 • Art. 6 High Risk (CE & Audits)
Tier 03 • Art. 50 Transparency Labels
Tier 04 • Art. 95 Minimal Risk (Free Use)
Tier 01 • Article 5 Unacceptable Risk (Banned)

Outlawed since 2 February 2025: 8 abusive AI practices that threaten human dignity, freedom, or democracy.

Banned • EUR 35M / 7% Inspect Tier
Art. 5(1)(a)–(h) • Total EU Ban on Biometric Surveillance & Social Scoring

8 Prohibited Practices (Art. 5(1)(a)–(h))

  • Subliminal manipulation (a) & exploiting vulnerable groups (b).
  • Public or private social scoring of citizens (c).
  • Crime prediction based solely on personality or profiling (d).
  • Mass facial scraping (e), workplace/school emotion scans (f) & sensitive biometric labelling (g).

Enforcement & Strict Warrant Exceptions

  • Total EU Market Ban: Cannot be sold, deployed, or used anywhere in the Union.
  • Live Public Facial ID (Art. 5(1)(h)): Banned for police unless a judge grants a prior warrant for missing victims, terrorism, or severe Annex II crimes.
  • Top Penalty: Up to EUR 35 million or 7% global turnover (Art. 99(3)).
Tier 02 • Art. 6 + Annexes I & III High Risk (Strict Safety Checks)

Allowed only after strict safety checks: AI inside regulated products (Annex I) and 8 sensitive life areas (Annex III).

Pre-Market Check & CE Inspect Tier
Arts. 6 & 14 • Medical, Aviation & Human-in-the-Loop Stop Button

Where High-Risk Rules Apply (Annex I & Annex III)

  • Regulated Products (Annex I, Art. 6(1) — 2 Aug 2027): Medical devices, diagnostics, aviation, cars, machinery, lifts, rail & toys.
  • 8 Sensitive Life Sectors (Annex III, Art. 6(2) — 2 Aug 2026): Biometrics, critical infrastructure, schools/exams, jobs & HR, credit & health/life insurance, policing, migration/borders & courts/elections.

Mandatory Protections (Arts. 8–17, 27, 43, 48 & 49)

  • Quality & Bias Controls (Arts. 9, 10, 17): Continuous risk management, QMS, and bias-checked training data.
  • Logs & Human "Stop Button" (Arts. 12 & 14): Automatic event logs (≥ 6 months) and trained humans able to override or stop the AI.
  • CE Mark, EU Database & FRIA (Arts. 15, 27, 48 & 49): Cybersecurity, CE certification, public EU Database listing, and deployer Fundamental Rights check.
Article 6(3) Practical Filter — When an Annex III Tool Is NOT High-Risk:
1. Narrow / Preparatory Tasks (Art. 6(3))

Exempt if the AI only performs a narrow procedural task, improves completed human work, spots patterns without replacing human judgement, or does preparatory work.

2. Profiling Is ALWAYS High-Risk

Crucial Counter-Exception: An Annex III system is always High-Risk whenever it profiles natural persons (scoring behaviour, location, or personal traits).

3. Document & Register (Arts. 6(4) & 49(2))

Providers claiming the Art. 6(3) filter must document their assessment before launch (Art. 6(4)) and still register in the EU Database (Art. 49(2)).

Tier 03 • Article 50 Transparency Risk (Clear Labels)

Honest AI labelling from 2 August 2026: Chatbots, synthetic media tools, and deepfakes must clearly identify themselves to you.

Mandatory Disclosure Inspect Tier
Art. 50(1)–(4) • Chatbot Disclosure, C2PA Watermarks & Clear AI Labels

Covered AI Tools

  • Customer chatbots and voice assistants (Art. 50(1)).
  • Generative AI creating synthetic text, audio, images, or video (Art. 50(2)).
  • Permitted emotion/biometric systems (Art. 50(3)) & deepfakes (Art. 50(4)).

How You Are Informed (Article 50)

  • Builder Duty (Art. 50(1)–(2)): Upfront chatbot notice and machine-readable watermarks (C2PA) embedded in AI outputs.
  • Deployer Duty (Art. 50(3)–(4)): Prior notice to individuals, visible deepfake badges, and labels on AI-written news (unless human-edited).
Tier 04 • Article 95 Minimal or No Risk (Free Everyday Use)

No new red tape for most everyday AI: Free EU market access with baseline staff AI Literacy (Art. 4).

Voluntary Codes + Art. 4 Inspect Tier
Arts. 4 & 95 • Free Everyday AI Tools, Spam Filters, Games & Voluntary Codes

Everyday Examples

  • AI video games and playlist/movie recommendation tools.
  • Email spam filters, spellcheckers, and internal search.
  • Factory predictive maintenance and inventory tools.

Simple Rules

  • Baseline Duty: Staff AI Literacy (Art. 4) plus existing GDPR and consumer safety laws.
  • Voluntary Codes (Art. 95): Optional green sustainability and accessibility pledges.

Priority 04 • DSM Directive (EU) 2019/790 & AI Act Arts. 51–56

Creators, Copyright & AI Training Rules

How Europe protects writers, artists, musicians, and publishers — giving creators the right to block AI web scraping and forcing AI labs to disclose their training data.

Arts. 51–56 AI Act & DSM Directive 01
Arts. 51–56 • GPAI Transparency & Systemic Safety

AI Training Rules, Open-Source & GPAI Models

Commercial AI labs can train on public web content only if creators have not opted out — and every GPAI provider (including open-source) must publish a training data summary.

Arts. 3–4 DSM Copyright Art. 53(2) Open-Source Rules > 10²&sup5; FLOPs Systemic Risk
See copyright rules, Open-Source duties & > 10²&sup5; FLOPs threshold
  • Scientific Research TDM (Art. 3 DSM / § 60d UrhG): Universities and research non-profits can mine lawfully accessed works for non-commercial science (no opt-out allowed).
  • Commercial AI Training (Art. 4 DSM / § 44b UrhG): Allowed only if creators have NOT opted out in a machine-readable format (Art. 4(3)).
  • Applies Even If Trained Outside Europe (Art. 53(1)(c)–(d) & Recital 106): Any GPAI model offered in the EU must respect EU copyright opt-outs (wherever trained) and publish a public training data summary.
  • Open-Source GPAI Rules (Art. 53(2)): Free open-source models (with public weights) are exempt from Annex XI/XII technical docs unless systemic — but MUST still obey copyright opt-outs (Art. 53(1)(c)) and publish the training summary (Art. 53(1)(d)).
  • Systemic-Risk Frontier Models (> 10²&sup5; FLOPs, Arts. 51(2), 52, 55 & 56): Models trained above 10²&sup5; FLOPs must notify the Commission within 2 weeks and run red-teaming, incident reporting, and cybersecurity checks (via Art. 56 Codes of Practice).

4 Real-World Court Cases & Enforcement Rulings

Key Takeaway + Case Details
Hamburg Regional Court (27 Sept 2024) § 60d UrhG Research

LAION v. Kneschke (Case 310 O 227/23)

Key Takeaway: Non-profit research datasets are protected under scientific TDM (§ 60d UrhG), and plain-text website Terms of Service can count as a valid commercial AI opt-out.

Read what the court decided

Hamburg judges dismissed a photographer’s claim against non-profit LAION under scientific research rules (§ 60d UrhG), while noting in obiter dicta that clear natural-language Terms of Service forbidding scraping qualify as “machine-readable” under Art. 4(3) DSM (§ 44b UrhG).

Munich Regional Court (2024–2025) Music & Memorisation

GEMA v. OpenAI & Suno

Key Takeaway: Tests whether AI models that memorise and output copyrighted song lyrics or music tracks go beyond TDM exceptions and require a paid licence.

Read what the case is about

German music society GEMA sued OpenAI (for reproducing protected song lyrics on simple prompts) and Suno (for synthetic audio mirroring hit songs), arguing that TDM permits pattern analysis — not memorising works to compete with artists unpaid.

UK High Court • Cross-Border IP Litigation Photos & Trademarks

Getty Images v. Stability AI

Key Takeaway: Challenges unlicensed scraping of 12M+ photos and trademark infringement when image generators reproduce distorted brand watermarks.

Read why this matters in Europe

Highlights two major risks: (1) training outside Europe does not escape EU law when models are offered here (closed in the EU by Art. 53(1)(c) and Recital 106), and (2) AI outputs showing garbled brand watermarks trigger trademark claims.

French Autorité de la Concurrence (2024) EUR 250M Sanction

French EUR 250M Fine on Google

Key Takeaway: Fined Google EUR 250M for training Gemini/Bard on news articles without telling publishers and forcing them to separated AI opt-out controls (Google-Extended).

Read the regulator’s decision

In Decision 24-D-03 (Art. 15 DSM press rights), France’s regulator ruled that tech platforms cannot force news outlets to disappear from Search just to block AI training — requiring separate AI opt-out controls (Google-Extended).

Article 113 (second paragraph) & Art. 113(b) (for Art. 101)

Full Application: High-Risk AI (Annex III), Deepfake Labels, Citizen Redress & GPAI Fines

The main rules take effect: Annex III High-Risk AI checks (jobs, credit, schools, public services), Art. 50 chatbot/deepfake labels, Citizen Redress Rights (Arts. 85–86), SME Sandboxes (Art. 57), and Art. 101 GPAI fines.

Article 111 Deadlines for Already-Existing (Legacy) AI Systems & Models:
Pre-Aug 2025 GPAI (Art. 111(3))

GPAI models placed on the EU market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027 to comply.

Pre-Aug 2026 High-Risk (Art. 111(2))

Exempt unless their design is significantly changed after 2 Aug 2026 — except public authority high-risk AI, which must comply by 2 August 2030.

EU Border/Justice IT (Art. 111(1))

Large-scale EU databases in Annex X (Schengen/Justice) in service before 2 Aug 2027 must comply by 31 December 2030.
